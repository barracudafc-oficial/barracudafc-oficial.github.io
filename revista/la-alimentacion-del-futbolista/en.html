Football nutrition

Energy to train, compete and grow
Wednesday, 4 November 2026 · Barracuda F.C.

Introduction

Football is much more than chasing a ball. It demands physical preparation, concentration, perseverance and healthy habits. Nutrition is essential because it supplies the energy and nutrients needed to train, compete, recover and develop properly.

From grassroots football to the professional game, every player should understand that eating well is part of sporting preparation.

At Barracuda F.C., educating athletes also means educating people for life. We encourage habits that support the physical and emotional wellbeing of children, teenagers and adult players.

A balanced diet alone cannot guarantee sporting success, but it is an important foundation for health and performance.

1. Why is good nutrition important?

During training or a match, players run, jump, accelerate, brake, change direction and concentrate. The body needs energy and nutrients from food to meet these demands.

Carbohydrates supply energy; proteins support growth and muscle repair; healthy fats provide energy and perform essential functions; vitamins and minerals support metabolism and muscle function; and water helps regulate body temperature and hydration.

Children and teenagers need enough varied food not only for sport but also for growth and development.

Key message: eating well is not about eating excessively, but about choosing enough varied foods to meet individual needs.

2. What should we eat before training or competing?

Training after many hours without food may reduce available energy and make concentration harder. Meal times should therefore be planned around sporting activities.

Suitable options include oats with fruit, bread with egg, rice with a protein source, plain yoghurt with fruit, and bananas or other familiar, easily digested foods.

When several hours remain before training, a full meal may be appropriate; closer to exercise, a light, familiar snack is often preferable. As a general guide, a main meal may be eaten around two to four hours beforehand, depending on its size and personal tolerance.

Avoid trying unfamiliar foods just before competition, as they may cause digestive discomfort. Barracuda tip: match preparation also starts with good nutrition.

3. Recovery after physical exertion

After training or a match, the body needs to replenish energy and fluids and obtain nutrients for muscle recovery. A post-exercise meal can combine carbohydrates, proteins and other nutritious foods.

Examples include rice with chicken and salad; fish with potato or cassava; an egg sandwich and fruit; plain yoghurt with oats and fruit; or lentils or beans with rice and a protein source.

Portion sizes differ according to age, growth, training intensity and individual characteristics. Recovery also requires sufficient rest, hydration and sensible scheduling.

Remember: training matters, but recovering properly is also part of sporting progress.

4. Fruit and vegetables: allies of our health

Fruit and vegetables provide vitamins, minerals, fibre and other beneficial substances.

Ecuador offers many options: bananas, papaya, mangoes, oranges, watermelon, pineapple, carrots, tomatoes and leafy greens.

Healthy eating need not be complicated or expensive. Local seasonal produce can contribute to a varied, balanced diet. Food should also be washed, stored and prepared hygienically.

Barracuda tip: choosing fruit and vegetables of different colours is a simple way to increase variety.

5. Nutrition and hydration: an inseparable team

Our previous article, “Hydration in sport”, explained why adequate fluid intake matters during exercise. Nutrition and hydration complement each other.

Water supports many bodily functions and replaces fluid lost through sweating. In hot areas such as Santa Elena province, environmental conditions require special attention.

Provide safe drinking water before, during and after training; schedule water breaks; pay attention to thirst and the weather; avoid strenuous activity when showing signs of dehydration; and report dizziness, weakness or discomfort to the coach immediately.

Fluid needs vary by person and activity. Long sessions or extreme heat may require additional precautions. Key message: good nutrition and hydration support safer sport.

6. Common nutrition mistakes among footballers

Some habits can harm dietary quality and make sporting preparation more difficult.

Skipping meals can make it harder to meet energy and nutrient needs. Frequently replacing proper meals with foods of low nutritional value reduces dietary variety.

Energy drinks are not the same as sports drinks: caffeinated or stimulant-containing energy drinks are not recommended for children and teenagers. Restrictive diets without professional advice can undermine health and growth.

Supplements do not replace balanced food and may carry risks without appropriate advice. Comparing bodies with those of other athletes is also unhelpful: needs differ, and weight or appearance should not dominate sports education.

Barracuda F.C. promotes respect for the body, self-respect and healthy habits.

7. The responsibility of parents and coaches

Nutrition education is a shared responsibility of players, families and coaches.

Families can provide enough varied food, arrange meals around school and training, ensure drinking water is available, set a healthy example and avoid negative comments about body weight or appearance.

Coaches can encourage hydration, consider rest and recovery, promote a healthy relationship with food, avoid prescribing diets or supplements beyond their expertise and refer families to qualified professionals when needed.

Sports education should provide a safe, respectful environment that supports overall development.

8. Nutrition is also part of sporting discipline

Discipline is often associated with punctuality, effort, following instructions and commitment to the team. Yet it also matters away from the pitch.

Getting enough sleep, eating balanced meals, drinking water and allowing recovery time all contribute to sporting preparation.

At Barracuda F.C., sporting values should accompany players throughout life. We aim not only to develop competitive footballers, but also responsible people who care for their wellbeing.

9. Five commitments to healthy eating

We invite our players and their families to make five commitments:

1. Eat a varied diet: include different food groups throughout the day.

2. Plan meals: fit eating times around sporting activities.

3. Stay hydrated: have safe drinking water available and drink according to individual needs.

4. Respect growth: avoid extreme diets, comparisons and unnecessary restrictions.

5. Learn together: seek reliable information and qualified health advice when in doubt.

Small everyday habits can stay with us for many years.

Final reflection

“A good footballer is not formed only on the pitch. They are also shaped by the decisions they make every day.”

Every training session is an opportunity to improve. Every balanced meal, adequate rest period and responsible decision helps protect our health.

True sporting education is measured not only in goals, victories or trophies, but also in values and habits.

Barracuda F.C. will continue promoting sporting and human education so that children, teenagers, women and men can develop through discipline, teamwork and respect.
